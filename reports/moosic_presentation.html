Automating Moosic Playlists

What clustering ~5,000 songs actually taught us — three algorithms, real failures, and one surprising correction.

Individual project, built in collaboration with Claude · Unsupervised (Machine) Learning Fundamentals
Where we started

5,235 songs. 13 raw audio features.

  • Correlation analysis found several features redundant
  • Loudness ≈ Energy + Acousticness combined
  • 7 features kept — each carrying independent signal
Correlation matrix — the actual feature set tested
Before the results

The real challenge: what does "good" even mean?

  • No labeled data — nothing tells an algorithm what a "correct" playlist looks like
  • No built-in success criteria — clustering math measures structure, not taste
  • "Good" is genuinely subjective — even our instructor confirmed dropping songs is fine, if what's left is coherent
  • Our answer: define success ourselves, up front — and always verify with a real, human listen on a sample
Method

How we built it

  • Tested 3 algorithms: K-Means, DBSCAN, Hierarchical
  • Success measured on two axes — coherence first, coverage second
  • Iterative process: tuned, tested, and corrected at every step
Algorithm 1

K-Means — the business-constrained workhorse

  • Elbow method alone suggested 3–5 clusters — mathematically "best," practically useless (1,000+ songs per playlist)
  • Overrode with business logic: target 20–60 songs per playlist
  • Result: ~130 playlists, ~98% landed inside the target range
  • Real limit found: "Teenage Dirtbag" landed in a dance-pop playlist — sound-alike on tempo/energy, not genre-alike
Strength

Full coverage. Playlist size fully controllable.

Limitation

Size target imposed by us, not discovered.

Elbow + silhouette — 3–5 looks "best," isn't usable
Final playlist sizes — 98% land in [20,60]
Algorithm 2

DBSCAN — honest about uncertainty

  • No fixed k — groups by density, flags outliers instead of forcing them in
  • Extensively tuned (eps, min_samples, 2 different scalers) — never found a workable middle ground
  • Every setting: one dominant mega-cluster plus noise, or fragmented pieces
  • Real value found anyway: correctly isolated a genuine classical/instrumental pocket, and a hidden rap/hip-hop pocket inside its own "noise"
Real sweep data — coverage climbs, silhouette never stays good
Strength

Strong diagnostic tool. Honest about what it doesn't know.

Limitation

Not viable as the primary playlist generator for this data.

Algorithm 3

Hierarchical — finding real coarse structure

  • No pre-set k — builds a full tree, we choose where to cut
  • Best-scoring cut (k=2–3) was the same misleading pattern as DBSCAN — one dominant group in disguise
  • k=8 gave 8 balanced, musically distinct groups
  • Independently re-found DBSCAN's two hidden pockets — cross-validated by a different method
8 supercluster sizes — balanced, no mega-cluster
Strength

Reveals real coarse structure. Strong hybrid potential with K-Means.

Limitation

Heavier to compute. Still needs a human-chosen cutoff.

One step further

A hybrid approach — and an honest correction

  • Tested: Agglomerative's 8 groups first, then K-Means split within each — songs never cross a broad category boundary
  • Size-target metrics barely moved (98.4% → 99.2%) — both pipelines already tuned to hit that target
  • 59% of single-stage playlists span more than one broad category — looked like a clear win for the hybrid

Then we checked one "worst offender" playlist by ear — it was a completely coherent jazz & bossa nova set the single-stage pipeline got right. The hybrid would have split it apart.

Neither approach wins outright — a real, demonstrated tradeoff
Our most important finding

What audio features can't capture

False positive

"Teenage Dirtbag"

Landed in a dance-pop playlist — matched on tempo and energy alone. The algorithm has no way to hear that it's a different genre entirely.

Caught in time

The jazz playlist

Flagged as "badly mixed" for spanning four categories. Checking the actual titles: Jobim, jazz standards — a completely coherent playlist, just sonically diverse.

These features track sound, not genre or mood the way people actually experience it.

The question

Is unsupervised machine learning the right approach? Yes — but only paired with an explicit, human-defined goal.

No algorithm has a concept of "good" on its own. Every useful result in this project came from combining the math with a clear decision about what we actually wanted.

Looking ahead

Where this goes next

More data that would help

  • Real listener behavior — skips, replays
  • Lyrics and language
  • Context — time of day, activity

Next steps

  • Validate with real listener feedback
  • Explore the hybrid approach further
  • A human-listening pass before production
The recommendation

Ship K-Means with business-defined playlist sizes — but keep a human in the loop, always.

Unsupervised learning: sorting by touch alone, blindfolded — with boxing gloves on.

Every algorithm we tested sorted honestly, by whatever it could feel. The features it had access to — and the ones it didn't — decided the rest.
